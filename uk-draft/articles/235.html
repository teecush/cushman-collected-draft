Theatre

ROBERT CUSHMAN's round-up of the London stage:-

Betrayal (Lyttelton): Harold Pinter's straightforward (though played backwards) triangle drama. This critic was disappointed, but there have been enough intelligent demurs for it to be worth your attention.

A Chorus Line (Drury Lane): Mostly exhilarating (and, by the way, superbly lit) musical now approaching the final chorus.

A Day in Hollywood, A Night in the Ukraine (New End): After regaling us with movie songs and anecdotes, a talented cast--notably Sheila Steafel as Harpo--play at being the Marx Brothers playing Chekhov. Accurate and therefore funny. Last week.

Filumena (Lyric): Barbara Jefford has temporarily replaced Joan Plowright as the fiery Italian ex-whore and eternal mother, and should be ideal.

Flashpoint (Mayfair): Gripping if not over-profound military melodrama set in Northern Ireland.

Night and Day (Phoenix): Erroneously hailed as a new departure for Tom Stoppard, it actually follows logically from his previous works. It presents a clash of principles: the feminine, and that of Press freedom.

The Ordeal of Gilbert Pinfold (Roundhouse): Michael Hordern is far too nice to be an acceptable Evelyn Waugh surrogate, but he is always worth watching on his own account and the production marks the welcome arrival in London of Manchester's Royal Exchange company.
