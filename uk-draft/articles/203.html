Theatre Guide

Ben Jonson to Bruce Forsyth

ROBERT CUSHMAN'S quick guide to London theatre.

BARTHOLOMEW FAIR (Young Vic): Slapdash production of Jonson's masterpiece that nevertheless makes the jokes funny and the moral clear.

CORIOLANUS (Aldwych): Strong performance by the RSC Brass Ensemble; Alan Howard blows the solo trumpet.

EVERY GOOD BOY DESERVES FAVOUR (Mermaid): The Stoppard-Previn parable of musical dissidence and political dissonance; probably the wittiest piece ever to be set in a mental hospital.

EVITA (Prince Edward): Hal Prince's production stunning, but the result still seems partially locked inside its authors' heads.

FLYING BLIND (Royal Court): A tragical farce about the farcical tragedy of Northern Ireland. Indispensable.

PLUNDER (Lyttelton): A revival of the revival of Ben Travers's 1920s farce; Dinsdale Landen the most cracking of gentlemen cracksmen.

ST MARK'S GOSPEL (Comedy): Last week of Alec McCowen's infinitely accomplished solo performance, before he goes to spread the word to the heathen of New York.

THE TRAVELLING MUSIC SHOW (Her Majesty's): Has only two more weeks to travel. Bruce Forsyth is worth seeing; and only masochists will regret that the show isn't really a Newley-Bricusse songbook.
