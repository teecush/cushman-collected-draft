Gypsy Triumphant

Robert Cushman · The Observer · The Observer Review · 1973-06-03
Theatre Review
Gypsy
VenuePiccadilly TheatreCityLondon
ActorAngela Lansbury

Gypsy triumphant

Something remarkable is currently happening at the Piccadilly Theatre. That is not opinion, it's fact: on Wednesday night I witnessed a standing ovation accorded to Angela Lansbury by a second night audience.

The cheers were provoked, I think, by the show's closing 15 minutes. For most of its length Gypsy is a very good musical; at its close, it reaches greatness. We have watched Miss Lansbury as a selfish and bone-stupid theatrical mother, steam-rollering her two luckless young daughters through the death throes of vaudeville. Gradually, but not grudgingly, we have come to admire her resilience, and to sympathise with her bewilderment as both girls desert her.

One runs off to become a real actress; the other achieves fake stardom as the stripper Gypsy Rose Lee, and immediately sets about banishing her mother from her dressing-room and her life.

Suddenly it's Mama's turn. A light-studded platform has been shunted across the orchestra pit, annihilating the distance that generally separates a musical from its audience. She strides on to it to try her own hand as a performer. She falters, weighted down by memories of the past, and her failure is Miss Lansbury's acting and singing triumph. She stops the show. The effect is devastatingly direct and as moving as anything to be seen in London.

Robert Cushman
