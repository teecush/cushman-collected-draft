Buddy Greco

by Robert Cushman

TEN YEARS AGO Buddy Greco was my favourite singer. He was furiously hip, and so made me feel that I was. But vested interests aside, his "Lady is a Tramp" was and remains a classic. Driving rhythm, some pleasingly mischievous tricks with the beat, and an enthusiasm that grows and feeds on itself through four choruses make it the definitive example of how to kid a standard. He still sings it: some people are beginning to think he wrote it.

Otherwise his repertory has changed. So has he. He has lost 30lb. and physically at least justifies his claim to the press that "we're into the whole youth thing now." Perhaps the Talk of the Town, with its stolidly middle-of-the-road audience, was not the best place to test the musical validity of this assertion. We got "For Once in My Life" and Jim Webb and Blood, Sweat and Tears -- all the semi-pop material on which the established cabaret singers have fastened with relief and with which they now hold court between two stools.

There was much to enjoy. He still sings up-tempo better than anyone. A number called "Keep the Customer Satisfied" did precisely that and his new one, "Everybody Gets to the Moon," should be a hit. He keeps the chat to a sensible minimum, dispenses it with charm, and is more relaxed than I would have thought possible.

The post-Sinatra mannerisms are gone, and probably they had to go. A sunny, infectious confidence remains but no wistfulness, no irony. And without these qualities the best new songs -- "Didn't We" and "Everybody's Talking" and a couple of bossa novas -- perforce become uninteresting big-voice ballads.
