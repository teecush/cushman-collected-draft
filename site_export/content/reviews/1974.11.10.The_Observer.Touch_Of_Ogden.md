Touch of Ogden

I WAS reading some of the poems of Ogden Nash before setting out for the Comedy, a theatre in London that I visit with a regularity that threatens to become monotonous;

Because nothing seems to run there and neither does the public, and Sunday critics are seated behind pillars which suggests that the management dislike or have forgotten us.

And this week's piece was called The Pay Off

And it was a comedy thriller written by William Fairchild, possibly when tired and in need of a lay-off.

And Nigel Patrick appeared in it as an eminent surgeon or slicer

Who had made so much money that he lived in a house full of borscht-coloured furniture whose aspect was applauded by the audience because they plainly could conceive of nothing nicer.

And this house was on the Sussex coast and the month was September and the day (for the play observed the unities) a Friday

Which turned out not to be Mr Patrick's day as neither indeed was it my day.

For it seems that this surgeon had antagonised the Treasury or rather incensed it

For he had placed his enormous earnings in a Swiss bank account without telling anyone, and there is a law, and what he was doing was against it.

And there were a famous actress and a pop singer (called, would you believe, Johnny Tomorrow) and they had done the same thing as Mr Patrick

Which made it a hat-trick.

And there was a fourth culprit played by the villainous Peter Vaughan and he was nastier than the others

Because they had invested their money abroad for noble reasons like trying to help their husbands and their secretaries and medical science and probably their grey-haired mothers.

Yes, they all claimed to have sinned with the best of intentions;

But Mr Vaughan was a money-grubber impure and simple and made no such pretensions.

In the first act they were all blackmailed by Peter Sallis, who is a very good actor, but in the second act we unfortunately saw much less of him

Because Mr Vaughan had made rather a mess of him.

Dulcie Gray played the actress and she kept delivering epigrams in the manner of the great Dame Edith

And they seemed inopportune and detrimental to suspense but you could not blame Miss Gray because the actress she followeth where her author and her director (who happened to be Mr Patrick) leadeth.

And the comedy was not comic and the thrills would not have excited the most secluded spinster

And I doubt if those responsible are going to be able to bank huge sums in Switzerland or even at their local branch of the National Westminster.

During the interval I asked one of my colleagues if the once respected firm of Tennents would have presented such stuff in the days of their late managing director Binkie Beaumont;

And he said he didn't think so for a meaumont.

Which seems to show where we are headed;

And makes one wonder just who can rescue the West End theatre from the mire in which it is embedded.

And Mr Fairchild's play does seem to contain a hint of the only possible measure:

We look to the Treasury however little it now has to treasure.

Robert Cushman
