Theatre-from A to W

ROBERT CUSHMAN'S quick guide to the London stage.

ANNIE (Victoria Palace): Triumphantly shameless musical--orphans, dogs, hissable villains, exhilarating scenery, unlimited knowhow.

* * *

BRAND (Olivier): Gruelling assault on an almost unscalable Ibsen; "worthy" has to be the word, though Robert Stephens's Mayor transcends it; the play itself is a rarity worth grabbing.

* * *

THE HOMECOMING (Garrick): Good Pinter revival in which the play comes over as unabashed black comedy.

* * *

PLENTY (Lyttelton): Fine performances from Kate Nelligan and Stephen Moore in David Hare's chronicle of a war heroine's failure to adjust to the next 10 years.

* * *

TEN TIMES TABLE (Globe): Alan Ayckbourn finally gets out of the house and into local politics.

* * *

WHOSE LIFE IS IT ANYWAY? (Savoy): Thoroughly gripping statement of the case for euthanasia transfers from the Mermaid. (At the Westminster, Malcolm Muggeridge is presenting the case against.)
