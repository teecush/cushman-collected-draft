Theatre round-up

ROBERT CUSHMAN'S quick guide to the London theatre

American Buffalo (Cottesloe): David Mamet, fairly new, fairly white American hope, makes poetry out of verbal and physical junk: some longueurs but an intelligent production with Dave King masterly.

* * *

The Dance of Death (Aldwych): Strindberg's marital tango rendered as funny as its fans have always claimed; Emrys James in fine bull-frog form.

* * *

Hindle Wakes (Greenwich): The founding regime at Greenwich goes out in glory with a Manchester classic.

* * *

Irish Eyes and English Tears (Theatre Upstairs): Nigel Baldwin is a playwright who has found his voice but not as yet his mouthpieces. See also "In at the Death" reviewed this week.

* * *

The Lady's Not For Burning (Old Vic): A necessary if disappointing revival. Fry can still be gloriously funny but the depths are non-existent.

* * *

Privates on Parade (Piccadilly): A fortnight left to see Peter Nichols's ENSA - and - after comedy, much reviled by the gay lobby who haven't noticed that all the characters are stereotypes, and meant to be Fun.

* * *

Savage Amusement (Warehouse): Vision of the near future, actually more 'savage' than amusing. Author Peter Flannery is the opposite of Nigel Baldwin: his story compels while his language flounders. David Threlfall is an actor to watch and impossible not to.

* * *

Every Good Boy Deserves Favour (Mermaid): The Stoppard-Previn parable of musical dissidence and political dissonance; probably the wittiest piece ever to be set in a mental hospital.
