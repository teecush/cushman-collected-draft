Arts Theatre: The English Opera Group

[Editorial note: This preview is unsigned. Its attribution to Robert Cushman is unconfirmed. It is included at the family's request as a Cambridge archival clipping, not as verified Robert Cushman authorship. No publication date is visible; the November dates in the text refer to performances, not publication.]

It is now recognised that Benjamin Britten is the greatest living opera composer in the world. During November Cambridge will have the chance to hear two of Britten's operas when the English Opera Group perform ALBERT HERRING and THE TURN OF THE SCREW next week. There will also be one performance of ENGLISH ECCENTRICS, an 'operatic entertainment' by the Australian composer Malcolm Williamson, which the English Opera Group commissioned for this year's Aldeburgh Festival.

The Group have just come back from a triumphant tour of Russia where they presented both these operas. They show Britten in two very different veins. ALBERT HERRING is a delicious comedy full of humour, as the inhabitants of a Suffolk village seventy years ago led by the formidable Lady Billows hunt for a suitable candidate to be May Queen, but in despair have to crown a May King—Albert Herring—as he is the only truly pure person in the village.

THE TURN OF THE SCREW which is closely based in the Henry James story, is a study of the forces of evil. Two children, Miles and Flora, live alone in a country house in Essex in the care of a housekeeper and a governess. The ghosts of the former valet and governess return to repossess the children whom they had corrupted in their lives. The housekeeper and the governess battle against the ghosts, but the price of their victory is Miles' death.

ENGLISH ECCENTRICS is drawn from Edith Sitwell's book of the same name where 114 characters were collected into an enthralling gallery of eccentricity. Malcolm Williamson has chosen nineteen of these, most of whom flourished during the middle years of the Regency. In seven different scenes the six artists involved appear over and over again in a series of delightful and delicious portrayals.

Thirty singers (including three boys) all of whom took part in the tour of Russia will be appearing in Cambridge. The English Chamber Orchestra will accompany the Group.

Benjamin Britten will himself conduct the performances of THE TURN OF THE SCREW on Friday November 13th and Saturday November 14th. Marcus Dods conducts ENGLISH ECCENTRICS; Bryan Balkwill conducts ALBERT HERRING and the performance of THE TURN OF THE SCREW on Tuesday November 10th.
