On stage

ROBERT CUSHMAN'S quick guide to London theatre.

In a desolate London scene one recent offering has been outstanding:--

WHOSE LIFE IS IT ANYWAY? (Mermaid, until Saturday; returns for a further run on 24 April): Brian Clark's gripping hospital piece asks whether or not a crippled Tom Conti should be allowed to take his own life. You almost expect the lights to go on, and the audience to be canvassed for opinions.

* * *

And one reasonable:--

KISMET (Shaftesbury): 25-year-old Turkish bath of a musical, old-fashioned even then, but tuppence-colourful and well sung.

* * *

A few golden oldies:--

THE ELOCUTION OF BENJAMIN FRANKLIN (Mayfair): Australia's most celebrated play; Gordon Chater's performance in Steve Spears's sparkling monologue of a transvestite is a tour-de-faiblesse.

* * *

FILUMENA (Lyric): De Filippo comedy, one of Naples' staples, given a funny and moving Zeffirelli production, with Joan Plowright and Colin Blakely.

* * *

MACBETH (Young Vic): The fabled Trevor Nunn-Ian McKellen-Judi Dench production transfers to a slightly larger theatre.

* * *

PRIVATES ON PARADE (Piccadilly): Life in a post-war troop concert-party, staged that way, too. Seems to have come out lighter than the author (Peter Nichols) intended, but no one else will worry. Taken all round, probably the most amusing show in town.

* * *

SIDE BY SIDE BY SONDHEIM (Garrick): The best theatre-songs of this generation, intelligently anthologised.
