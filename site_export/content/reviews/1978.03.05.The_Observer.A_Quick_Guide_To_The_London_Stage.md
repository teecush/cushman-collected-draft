A quick guide to the London stage

THEATRE

A QUICK guide to the London stage:-

A BED OF ROSES (Royal Court from 14 March): The improvising group Hull Truck in its most complex play yet. Excellent songs make it almost a musical - the new British genre.

THE CHERRY ORCHARD (Olivier): Half-successful production, graced by Dorothy Tutin, Albert Finney, Ben Kingsley, Susan Fleetwood, and a nippy new translation by Michael Frayn.

THE ELOCUTION OF BENJAMIN FRANKLIN (Mayfair): Gordon Chater's tour-de-force solo in a wittily written Australian piece about the inoffensive 'transvestite terror' of Sydney; innocent but confined.

PRIVATES ON PARADE (Piccadilly): Improved transfer of Peter Nichols's concert-party memoirs; possibly the funniest show in London, and the best of the almost-musicals. Denis Quilley plays gay, and Nigel Hawthorne very straight.

SAINT JOAN (Old Vic): Eileen Atkins defying hell and breasting high water to deliver a wholly consistent interpretation, backed by a sensible production.

THE REAR COLUMN (Queen's): Caught in the Congo, somewhere between 'Journey's End' and 'Waiting for Godot,' five Victorian officers in Simon Gray's latest go to pieces, victims of the heat, the natives, their absent commander, H. M. Stanley and each other. Handsome and sort of civilised, but long.

STREAMERS (Roundhouse): More military, Americans this time, waiting for Vietnam while doing a better job on one another than the Cong ever could. Our strongest introduction yet to David Rabe, writer of plays and winner of prizes.

Robert Cushman
