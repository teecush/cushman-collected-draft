Robert Cushman's quick guide to the London stage

THE BUNDLE (Warehouse): Masterly parable play by Edward Bond, with eye-catching performance by Mike Gwilym.

* * *

DINGO (Warehouse): Charles Wood's anti-war argument is now familiar and accepted, but his stagecraft can still surprise: meet the real Desert Rats.

* * *

FROZEN ASSETS (Warehouse): London's busiest theatre can still accommodate Barrie Keeffe's Christmas tour of the haves and have-nots, which may have been bitterly conceived but plays with ineffable cheerfulness.

* * *

THE GUARDSMAN (Lyttelton): Molnar's study of jealousy and role-playing (among actors, what a surprise) is plainly nothing like a masterpiece, but, with Diana Rigg on the premises, some of the glamour and wit of old Hungary survives.

* * *

KINGDOM COME! (King's Head): Caribbean allegory of the state of Ulster; possibly perceptive and certainly tuneful.

* * *

MEMOIR (Ambassadors): Sarah Bernhardt remembers, with the fitful, fretful aid of a male secretary; as duologues go, a fizzer. Author (John Murrell) Canadian; actors (Siobhan McKenna, Niall Buggy) Irish.

* * *

WATERS OF THE MOON (Haymarket): Back to its old home, via Chichester, comes N. C. Hunter's compassionate piece of Aid to Distressed Gentlefolk proving the fifties were not as bad as they have been cracked down to be. Wendy Hiller remains from 28 years ago, though in a different role; Ingrid Bergman is the glamorous new arrival.
