Theatre

A CHORUS LINE (Drury Lane): And this week it's the final chorus--or, if you prefer, the end of the Line.

A FAIR QUARREL (Olivier): Ascetic revival of an interesting curio, shedding light on duelling, smoking, enforced marriage, and other Jacobean customs. Acting underpowered at the premiere, but hope springs.

FLASHPOINT (Mayfair): Lively barrack-room melodrama set during the Anglo-Irish war which, in case you're wondering, happened--like so much else in current drama--next year. Last week.

LARK RISE (Cottesloe): Coming up for the third time, a charming combination of epic and pastoral, set in Victorian Oxfordshire around (honest, it's in the book) Cottisloe Pond.

NORMAL SERVICE (Hampstead): Pleasant comedy set in the design-room of a Scottish TV company that will rank (let us hope) among coming playwright John Byrne's lesser works.

SUBJECTS FOR INTERROGATION (Bush): Twin plays by new writer Frederick Harrison on the rise of the British torturer; the first windy and worthless; the second ingenious and, to be disrespectfully candid, fun.
